# Why the Cao coastline coverage changes at 410 Ma

Reviewed 2026-09-12. This record explains the source-layer boundary; regional
correction records separately qualify material, geometry and placement.

The Merdith et al. (2021) model supplement describes its coastline collection as
mainly covering the last 400 Ma, whereas continental shapes cover 1 Ga. Its
accepted manuscript, Figure 11, explicitly compares the older Domeier models
ending at 410.1 Ma with the younger reconstruction beginning at 410 Ma. Figures
9–11 distinguish modeled continental lithosphere, inferred older lithosphere
with uncertain evidence or later deformation, and schematic continental extent.
Those categories are not reconstructed shorelines or proof of surface exposure.

Cao et al. (2024), section 2.1, explicitly adopt Merdith's model for 1000–0 Ma,
with adjustments focused on the Neoproterozoic. The paper also acknowledges
abrupt appearances of some blocks at older model-coverage limits because their
earlier extent lacks sufficient evidence. That general caveat is consistent
with a coverage interpretation, but is not a feature-specific explanation of
the 410 Ma records.

This documented junction matches the independently reproduced Cao v2.4
coastline activation boundary. Our inference is that the regional appearance
reflects inherited differences in coverage and representation, not continental
creation at 410 Ma. A particular feature's ancestry still requires its own
identity and geometry check; matching dates alone do not establish lineage.

## Additional native geometry inspected

The pinned Cao v2.4 archive also contains `COBfile_1800_0.gpml`, 14,423,254 bytes,
SHA-256 `cfcea20c5244613e53ad4b9cdf6411d79ff535c25af335b4fa6eb9251377d4bd`.
It is distinct from the already compiled coastline collection. Original source
rights and frame remain those recorded in the Cao foundation provenance.

Inspection found closed continental boundaries named Laurentia aCOB (plate
101, 1070–410.1 Ma), Baltica aCOB (302, 1000–0 Ma), West Svalbard (309,
600 Ma onward), East Svalbard (311, 600 Ma onward), and Pearya South (121,
1000 Ma onward). Other regional COB entries retain the 410 Ma cutoff.
The abbreviation `aCOB` has not been independently defined in the consulted
documentation; it is retained literally, without an invented expansion.

A collection name, old validity range or modern-coordinate intersection does
not alone qualify an older correction. Regional compilers must establish exact
source identity, same-frame reconstructed overlap, material affinity and
duplication against active older geometry. Existing older representation counts
as material continuity; it must not be rendered again as a separate continent.

## Western partitions also contain younger crust

The independently acquired USGS basement-domain map, Lund et al. (2015),
[Data Series 898](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ds898), DOI 10.3133/ds898,
distinguishes crust-formation age from later accretion. Its Table 1 and spatial
attributes show that the broad Cao polygons named East Klamath and Purcell
contain substantial younger basement domains. Regional intersection evidence
is recorded separately in the western audit; a source name does not establish
that its complete footprint belongs to the named terrane.

This changes the repair method. The task requires age-qualified treatment of
the younger partition as well as filling independently supported older gaps.
An older schematic aCOB overlapping younger mapped basement does not establish
ancestry of that younger basement. It can represent a different inferred older
extent, with its own uncertainty. Local corrections must not turn that spatial
coincidence into continuity of the same material.

DS898 is a 2015 USGS national-scale basement compilation for the conterminous
United States and Alaska at 1:5,000,000, consulted and acquired 2026-09-12. The
western source record pins the actual service response, geometry, rights and
age-field exceptions. Its modern reference geometry and formation-age ranges
alone do not determine ancient exposed land, the geometry of a partially
formed domain, or an exact palaeoposition.
